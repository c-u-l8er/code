A mode is consulted where work starts, never where it
      continues. Nothing here stops a worker that is already running: a goal mid-flight
      is a commitment already made, and killing it leaves a worktree of uncommitted work
      with nobody to review it. Reading, reviewing, findings and reports are never gated
      — a lane that stops reporting has gone dark, which is much harder to notice
      than one that is paused.

Mission —

    
What this workspace is for, in your words. It goes out with the
    doctrine to every plan, every worker and every review, and it is the only text
    here no agent may write. The doctrine says how work is done anywhere in
    this ecosystem; this says what this workspace is for.

    
    

      
      
    


    
Alignment

    
The supervisor reads the mission, the doctrine, every goal in
    every lane, what is proposed, what the work has reported back, which standing
    obligations are failing, and what is running - and says whether that is still
    the mission. It recommends. It never stops, starts or closes anything.

    

      
      
    

    


    
Workspaces

    
A workspace is a separate board: its own lanes, goals, consults,
    findings, obligations, direction and worktrees. Switching is refused while
    anything is running, because every path this console writes to moves with it.

    

    

      
      
      
    

    
Move a lane

    
A lane belongs to one workspace, and so do its goals and its
    worktree — so moving it moves all three, and uncommitted work in the
    worktree comes with it. What is not moved is the history written about
    the lane elsewhere: findings, proposals and reports stay with the workspace that
    recorded them. Ask first, and it says how many of each before you decide.

Report

    
One page for the whole workspace: what has happened since the last
    report, what is running right now, and where this is headed — with the lanes,
    the standing problems, and everything the work has reported back. It is a file, not
    a view: it keeps, it opens without this console, and the next report is measured
    against it.

    

    

      
      
      
    

    

    
Taken so far

    
Solving a report reads its numbers back — the ones on disk, not
    today's — and writes what it makes of them into the proposal queue, scored on the
    same four axes and held by the same bars and gates as anything else. It adopts nothing
    and moves no goal: everything it says still has to be taken up by hand.

    

    

    

  






  

    
Settings


    
Who does what

    
Three roles, three models, three switches. These are the
    lights in the header. Off means refused, not hidden: a role you switch
    off here stops running, and the thing that would have used it says so.

    

    
Every role lists every model. opus, sonnet and
    haiku run on your Claude subscription; codex runs
    codex exec against your ChatGPT one. Neither spends metered
    money. luna, terra and sol are the paid OpenRouter tiers,
    cheapest first. A supervisor set to architect follows whatever the
    architect is, so you only change it in one place.

    
A greyed-out option is one that role cannot do at all, and
    it says why on the line. One that names a problem in brackets can still be
    picked — that is a sign-in away from working, not a dead end.

    
    
Off means nothing in this console can spend OpenRouter credit.
    Reading your remaining balance is a free request and keeps working.

    

    
    
Start a proposed goal without asking me

    
A proposal has to clear both of these to start on its own. Below
    either one it waits in Direction with its reason showing.

    

      
      
      
    

    
Every done-condition met, without stopping to ask you for something.
    0% adopts anything that has been scored at all; 100% means you decide every goal.

    

      
      
      
    

    
Scored against the rung each lane's evidence has actually reached, not
    against how good the idea is. Without this bar the harness reliably starts the safest
    work it can find rather than the work the mission is waiting on — tidying always
    looks likelier to finish than a deploy. What it costs is deliberately not a bar:
    it ranks proposals against each other, and the ceiling on spending is the daily one.

    


    
Where your work is kept

    
Nothing here has ever lived in the browser — the board,
    this chat, every finding, goal and ruling are files on your disk. Alongside
    them is a SQLite database holding a second copy of every one, plus the
    previous versions, so a bad write is something you can look back past and a
    backup is one file rather than a directory.

    
reading…

    
    
Off leaves only the JSON files. The files are the ones that
    are read, always — the database is the copy, so switching this off
    cannot lose anything you have now, only the history from here on.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    
A save copies itself as it happens. The sweep is for the
    files nothing saves through — packets, rulings, reports, consult
    transcripts. 0 turns it off and leaves the button below.

    

      
      
      
      
    

    


    
Cloud backup

    
Not connected to anything yet, and nothing leaves this
    machine. What is already built for it is the part that is hard to add later:
    every change is numbered, so a sync only ever has to ask what has
    happened since number N. Your credentials are the one thing deliberately
    kept out of this database, so what would be uploaded contains no key.
