Mission —

What this workspace is for, in your words. It goes out with the doctrine to every plan, every worker and every review, and it is the only text here no agent may write. The doctrine says how work is done anywhere in this ecosystem; this says what this workspace is for.

Alignment

The supervisor reads the mission, the doctrine, every goal in every lane, what is proposed, what the work has reported back, which standing obligations are failing, and what is running - and says whether that is still the mission. It recommends. It never stops, starts or closes anything.

Workspaces

A workspace is a separate board: its own lanes, goals, consults, findings, obligations, direction and worktrees. Switching is refused while anything is running, because every path this console writes to moves with it.

Settings

Who does what

Three roles, three models, three switches. These are the lights in the header. Off means refused, not hidden: a role you switch off here stops running, and the thing that would have used it says so.

Every role lists every model. opus, sonnet and haiku run on your Claude subscription; codex runs codex exec against your ChatGPT one. Neither spends metered money. luna, terra and sol are the paid OpenRouter tiers, cheapest first. A supervisor set to architect follows whatever the architect is, so you only change it in one place.

A greyed-out option is one that role cannot do at all, and it says why on the line. One that names a problem in brackets can still be picked — that is a sign-in away from working, not a dead end.

Off means nothing in this console can spend OpenRouter credit. Reading your remaining balance is a free request and keeps working.

Start a proposed goal without asking me

A proposal has to clear both of these to start on its own. Below either one it waits in Direction with its reason showing.

Every done-condition met, without stopping to ask you for something. 0% adopts anything that has been scored at all; 100% means you decide every goal.

Scored against the rung each lane's evidence has actually reached, not against how good the idea is. Without this bar the harness reliably starts the safest work it can find rather than the work the mission is waiting on — tidying always looks likelier to finish than a deploy. What it costs is deliberately not a bar: it ranks proposals against each other, and the ceiling on spending is the daily one.
